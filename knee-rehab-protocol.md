# Knee Rehabilitation Protocol
**Post-Meniscus Surgery — 6-Week Progressive Loading Plan**

This protocol runs in parallel with every session in the training plan. It has three components:
1. **Pre-run activation** — 5 min before every run (Mon, Fri, Sat)
2. **Knee-specific strength block** — inserted into every Wednesday gym session
3. **Post-session recovery** — 5–8 min after every session

---

## Red Flags — Stop Training Immediately

Do not push through any of the following. Contact your physio.

- Sharp, stabbing or electric pain *inside* the joint (not muscle soreness)
- Swelling or warmth around the knee that persists more than 24 hours after activity
- A locking, clicking, or catching sensation under load
- Pain that forces you to change your normal gait while running
- Pain that does not resolve fully overnight before the next session

Muscle soreness and mild aching around the knee is normal and expected. Joint pain is not.

---

## Pre-Run Activation (5 min — before EVERY run)

Do these in order before every Monday, Friday, and Saturday session. They fire up the VMO, glutes, and hip stabilisers before impact loads arrive.

| Exercise | Reps / Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Clamshells (band) | 15 each side | Keep hips stacked, rotate from hip not waist |
| Terminal Knee Extension (TKE, band) | 15 each leg | Loop band around rack at knee height, step in, slight knee bend, extend to full lock |
| Glute Bridge | 20 reps | Drive through heels, squeeze glutes at top, 2s hold |
| Single-leg balance | 30s each leg | Eyes open Wks 1–2; eyes closed Wks 3–6 |
| Mini lateral band walk | 10 steps each direction | Band just above ankles, stay low, toes forward |

> **Why TKEs matter:** The VMO (inner quad) is the single most important muscle for post-meniscus knee stability. It atrophies rapidly after surgery. TKEs reload it specifically without stressing the joint.

---

## Wednesday Knee Block — By Phase

Each Wednesday gym session includes a dedicated knee block *before* the main strength work. Do it first — before fatigue sets in — so quality is high.

---

### PHASE 1 — Weeks 1 & 2: Neuromuscular Reactivation

**Goal:** Re-establish muscle firing patterns. The knee has lost proprioception (spatial awareness) and motor control from surgery and 3+ months of reduced loading. Fix this before adding weight.

**Do 3 rounds. Rest 45–60s between rounds.**

| Exercise | Reps | Cues |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal Knee Extension (TKE) | 20 each leg | Full extension, pause 1s at lockout, slow return |
| Short Arc Quad (SAQ) | 15 each leg | Towel roll under knee at ~30°, extend to full lock, 2s hold. Quad only — no deep bend |
| Straight Leg Raise | 15 each leg | Lying flat, keep leg straight, raise to 45°, 2s hold, 3s lower |
| Clamshell (band) | 20 each side | Slow and controlled, top hip stays stacked |
| Single-leg Balance | 45s each leg | Slight knee bend, not locked out. Eyes open. |
| Heel Slide | 15 each leg | Lying flat, slide heel toward glutes through full comfortable range. Do not force. |

**Total: ~12 min**

**What to expect:** The surgical knee will feel weaker and less coordinated than the other leg. Heel slides may be uncomfortable at deep flexion — stop before pain, not through it. This gap will narrow noticeably within 10–14 days.

---

### PHASE 2 — Weeks 3 & 4: Progressive Loading

**Goal:** Build load-bearing strength through functional ranges. Introduce eccentric control — the most important adaptation for running deceleration and stair descent, and the biggest gap after meniscus repair.

**Do 3 rounds. Rest 60–75s between rounds.**

| Exercise | Reps | Cues |
|---|---|---|
| TKE (heavier band) | 20 each leg | Move up one band resistance from Phase 1 |
| Step-Down (eccentric) | 12 each leg | Stand on 20–30cm box, lower non-stance heel slowly to floor (3–4 count), return. This is the key eccentric loading exercise. |
| Single-leg Glute Bridge | 12 each leg | Keep hips level, don't let opposite side drop |
| Nordic Hamstring Curl (assisted) | 8 reps | Partner holds ankles or loop band overhead. Lower slowly (3–4 count), pull back up with assistance. Eccentric only. |
| Lateral Band Walk | 20 steps each direction | Maintain slight squat, controlled tempo |
| Wall Sit | 45s | 90° at knee. Add 10s each week. |
| Single-leg Calf Raise | 15 each leg | Slow eccentric down (3 count). Use wall for balance. |

**Total: ~15 min**

**Why the step-down:** Every running stride involves your knee decelerating your body weight through mid-stance. If the quad can't control that eccentrically, the load goes to the meniscus. The step-down is the single best exercise to train this pattern.

**Why Nordic hamstrings:** The hamstring assists in stabilising the tibia under the femur — critical after meniscus repair. Eccentric loading (the lowering phase) is where the adaptation happens.

---

### PHASE 3 — Weeks 5 & 6: Sport-Specific Strength

**Goal:** Replicate the forces of Hyrox — single-leg landings, loaded carries, lunges with a tired knee. Build confidence for race day.

**Week 5: 3 rounds. Rest 75s. Week 6: 2 rounds only (taper).**

| Exercise | Reps | Cues |
|---|---|---|
| TKE (heaviest band) | 20 each leg | Now automatic — use this as a warm-up within the block |
| Single-leg Box Squat (to box) | 10 each leg | Sit back to a box (40–50cm), controlled 3-count descent, drive up through heel |
| Step-Down — increased height | 12 each leg | Raise box to 35–40cm |
| Nordic Hamstring Curl (less assist) | 8 reps | Reduce assistance. Go as far as you can before breaking form. |
| Spanish Squat (wall sit with band) | 60s | Band around rack at knee height, sit back into it — loads quad without tibial shear |
| Reverse Lunge (DBs) | 10 each leg | Step backward, not forward — safer kneecap tracking |
| Single-leg Calf Raise — weighted | 12 each leg | Hold a DB, slow 3-count eccentric |

**Total: ~16 min (Week 5), ~10 min (Week 6)**

**Week 6 note:** Only 2 rounds of this block on Wednesday June 25. Race week is about staying sharp, not accumulating new fatigue.

## Post-Session Recovery (5–8 min — after EVERY session)

Do this after every run and every gym session without exception.

| Exercise | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standing quad stretch | 60s each leg | Pull heel to glute, keep knees together, stand tall |
| Lying hamstring stretch | 60s each leg | Towel around foot, leg straight |
| Hip flexor lunge stretch | 75s each side | Back knee on ground, reach overhead, slight back bend |
| IT band foam roll | 60s each leg | From hip to just above knee. Slow. |
| Calf stretch on step | 60s each leg | Heel hanging off step, straight knee then bent knee |
| Ice (if needed) | 10–15 min | Only if you notice swelling or heat in the joint. Wrap ice in cloth, never direct. |

## Knee Loading — Weekly Volume Tracking

Use this to self-monitor your knee load across the 6 weeks. If the surgical knee is at >5/10 pain on any day, reduce load the next session and do not skip the recovery protocol.

| Week | Knee Block volume | Running days | Max acceptable knee discomfort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Light — 3 rounds activation | 3 | 2–3/10 dull ache only |
| 2 | Light — 3 rounds activation | 3 | 3/10 |
| 3 | Moderate — 3 rounds loading | 3 | 3–4/10 max during exercise |
| 4 | Moderate-heavy — 3 rounds | 3 | 4/10 max |
| 5 | Heavy — 3 rounds sport-specific | 3 | 3/10 (we are not adding fatigue pre-race) |
| 6 | Taper — 2 rounds only | 2–3 | 2/10 max. Any higher = rest. |

---

## Signs of Good Adaptation (What to Look For Each Week)

- **Week 2:** Heel slides reaching fuller range without discomfort. Single-leg balance feeling more solid.
- **Week 3:** Step-down feels controlled. Surgical knee strength approaching the other side on TKEs.
- **Week 4:** Nordic hamstring curl feels strong. No swelling after Saturday simulations.
- **Week 5:** Single-leg box squat feels confident. Running gait symmetrical — no favouring of one side.
- **Race week:** Knee feels like part of your body again, not something to manage.
